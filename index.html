How I run an AI bet

Frame the unknown, test it small, decide before the squad commits.

This maps directly to Lifeblood's Exploration Framework, from Discovery through Prove. I keep feasibility in front of features: the fastest way to waste a squad is to build for six weeks on an assumption nobody tested.

01 · Discovery

Frame the unknown

Name the single riskiest assumption. Usually technical: can the data actually support this, fast enough, safely enough.

02 · Discovery

Spike it

Smallest possible build, often a low-fidelity AWS prototype, scoped to answer that one question and nothing else.

03 · Evidence

Read the result

Model evaluation, latency, data quality and lineage. Synthesise it into something a non-technical stakeholder can act on.

04 · Gate

Go / No-Go

A DVF call on evidence: desirable, viable, feasible. Including the level of human oversight an AI-assisted decision needs.

05 · Prove

Build & scale

Only after the gate clears. Sequence the backlog, manage dependencies, hold the line on safety and quality.

The coral gate repeats across every product below. Signal comes in, a human decides. That is the discipline the role is built around.

Technical fluency

I speak the engineers' language. I don't need to be one.

The role asks for enough technical understanding to engage architects credibly, assess feasibility, and translate constraints into decisions. Here's where that fluency actually sits.

RAG architecture

Refusal rules, out-of-scope handling, and the exact point where the model has to hand off to a human. Written as acceptance criteria.

Cloud delivery (AWS)

Governance & oversight

Human-in-the-loop as a hard rule, not a slogan. I set the level of oversight an AI-assisted decision needs before it can be relied on.

Why this role, specifically

I've already made trade-off calls inside a blood supply chain. That's not theory for me.

The forecasting bridge

My undergraduate research forecast ATM cash demand: get a perishable, time-sensitive resource to the right place before it's needed, without over-stocking. That's the same problem shape as blood supply against a 42-day shelf life. Different domain, identical maths.

Blood bank domain credibility

Safety over speed, for real

I've delivered where an AI output had to be independently validated and where a suggestion couldn't act without a clinician. A safety-first, values-led environment isn't a culture shock. It's how I already work.
